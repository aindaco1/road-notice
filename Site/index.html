A heads-up before the camera.

Road Notice uses your location and a saved camera list to give one brief siren per approach to a listed speed or red-light camera, when location and audio conditions permit. It cannot detect radar signals or confirm whether a camera is operating. Use it alongside your usual navigation app; there is no map to watch.

  • Background warnings. With Always and Precise Location approved, monitoring can continue while another app is open or the screen is locked, when iOS permits. Reopen Road Notice after restarting your phone or swiping it away.
  • Your usual car audio. The siren uses the iPhone speaker, connected Bluetooth or CarPlay audio, including in Silent mode. It uses media volume; calls, a muted stereo or an interrupted connection can keep you from hearing it.
  • Quiet below the limit. On by default. Speed-camera sirens stay quiet when the phone reliably reports a speed below the camera’s known or estimated limit. Unknown or uncertain information still gets a warning. Red-light and combined-camera warnings stay on.
  • Works without a mobile signal. A camera list is saved on your phone. Internet access is needed for updates and sending a problem report, not for checking saved cameras.
  • Tell me when something breaks. Version 1.0.1 adds a reviewed problem report with an optional explanation and technical details. Here’s how it works.

Background location uses battery. Road Notice keeps monitoring requested in Low Power Mode, but iOS, GPS reception and audio conditions can still delay or prevent a warning. It cannot guarantee every camera or every alert.

Set it up while you’re parked.

  1. Turn on Camera warnings.

    Open Road Notice and switch Camera warnings on. At the first location prompt, choose Allow While Using App, with Precise enabled. Avoid Allow Once: it does not give the app lasting access.

  2. Give it background location access.

    If iOS asks to change access to Always, approve it. Otherwise open Settings → Privacy & Security → Location Services → Road Notice. Select Always and turn Precise Location on. Location Services must also be on at the top of that settings page.

    If Always isn’t listed yet, return to the app, enable warnings and approve the first location request, then check Settings again. If you previously chose Don’t Allow, change it here. The app cannot approve permission for you.

  3. Allow notifications and sound.

    Choose Allow at the notification prompt. Then check Settings → Notifications → Road Notice: turn on Allow Notifications, Sounds and Time Sensitive Notifications where available. Allow Lock Screen and banner alerts if you want to see them.

    If you use a Focus, check Settings → Focus and allow Road Notice and time-sensitive notifications where those options appear. Driving Focus has different controls; test with the Focus you actually use. Apple’s Focus instructions.

  4. Allow camera-list updates.

    In Settings → General → Background App Refresh, enable Road Notice. Choose Wi-Fi & Cellular Data and allow the app cellular access if you want downloads away from Wi-Fi. Low Power Mode disables Background App Refresh; you can still use Update now inside the app.

  5. Test the sound in your car.

    Connect Bluetooth or CarPlay, select the correct stereo input, and tap Test warning. Start at a comfortable volume, then raise the phone’s media volume and the car volume until the short siren is easy to hear. Try Silent mode too. The ringtone-volume setting is separate.

    If sound comes from the wrong place, check the output shown beneath Test warning and the audio-output control in Control Center. A sound test checks your current audio connection, not future background GPS reception.

  6. Check the list and Status.

    Tap Update now while connected. Confirm the database shows warning locations and a publication date. Status should show Monitoring or Ready · waiting for movement once a usable location is available. Resolve permission or database messages first. Then leave Camera warnings on.

The app needs location and notifications. It does not request camera, microphone, contacts, photos, or Motion & Fitness access. Ordinary car-audio playback has no separate Bluetooth permission prompt.

iPhone. iOS 17 or later.

Works on iPhone XS, XS Max, XR and newer, plus iPhone SE (2nd generation and later). Supports iOS 17, 18, 26 and 27. Check Settings → General → About for your model and iOS version.

Something not working?

I passed a camera and heard nothing.

When parked, check Camera warnings, Always and Precise Location, then try Test warning on the same audio connection. Tap Update now. With Quiet below speed limit on, silence may be expected. Missing cameras, poor GPS, direction of travel and interrupted monitoring can also explain it.

The siren is silent or uses the wrong speaker.

Raise media volume, check the output beneath Test warning and select the right car-stereo input. End any call and try again. A Bluetooth connection can send sound to the car even when you expect the phone speaker.

I’m below the posted limit but it still warns.

The app may have an uncertain speed, an unknown limit or a lower estimate for the road. School-zone cameras may use the reduced limit throughout the day. Red-light and combined cameras always warn. Follow posted signs; a siren does not establish that you were speeding.

How do I stop it?

Turn off Camera warnings to stop location monitoring. Permissions can also be changed in iPhone Settings. For a permission problem, follow the setup steps.

Found something weird? Tell me.

While parked, open Road Notice and tap Report a problem. Tell me what happened, add anything that might help, then review and send. Your reports help me make the app better.

Nothing is sent automatically. Reports are public, so leave out personal information. What’s included?

The camera list.

Road Notice combines OpenStreetMap with public city, county and transportation-agency records. Albuquerque and its surrounding metro get particular attention, alongside selected major U.S. metros. Coverage is incomplete and varies by place.

Possible speed camera means the position or current equipment presence is uncertain. Some records cover a stretch of road or a known mobile-camera deployment area. They are not live sightings. Separate travel directions can count as separate warning locations.

Limits come from agency records, community data and matching cameras to roads. Some are estimates. Where several supported limits might apply, the app uses the lower one to decide whether to stay quiet. It cannot see school beacons, temporary signs or changing road conditions.

Source checks run Sunday at 9 p.m. Mountain Time, before publication at midnight as Sunday becomes Monday. The schedule follows Denver daylight saving time. Your phone catches up when iOS and its connection allow it. Failed downloads keep the saved list in place.

Your driving stays on your phone.

Location and speed are used on your iPhone to check for cameras. They are not attached to camera-update requests or the automatic details in a problem report. There are no accounts, ads, tracking or automatic analytics uploads.

What stays on the phone

The app keeps settings, camera lists and recent encounter identifiers and times to avoid repeated sirens. Local diagnostics include the latest sound attempt, which can name a camera and audio output. The app does not record a continuous trip log.

Version 1.0.1 also keeps a limited, location-free troubleshooting history for up to 24 hours, and up to five filtered Apple crash or hang diagnostics and five report drafts for seven days. This reporting folder is excluded from backups. Use Delete local report history inside Report a problem to clear it.

What you choose to send

Only tapping Send report uploads a reviewed report. It can contain app and iOS versions, iPhone model, permissions, recent warning decisions, audio and database status, selected crash details and your explanation. Reports pass through the Cloudflare-hosted service at crash.dustwave.xyz and become public GitHub issues. There is no advertising or tracking use.

The service keeps retry receipts for 30 days and bounded summaries to group reports. Public issues may remain indefinitely. A report can identify you if you put identifying information in it; it is not guaranteed anonymous. Deleting local history does not remove published reports, and removing an issue cannot erase copies others have made.

Connections and your choices

The website and camera downloads use GitHub Pages and Cloudflare. Those providers receive normal connection information, including IP addresses, under their Cloudflare and GitHub privacy policies. The reporting service uses short-lived keyed hashes for abuse limits; it does not put IP addresses in issues. Apple handles any information shared through TestFlight.

You can stop monitoring, change permissions, discard drafts or delete the app. Other local app data may be included in backups according to your settings. For privacy questions, contact Alonso Indacochea through support; do not post private information publicly.

You’re still the driver.

Road Notice is an awareness aid, not permission to speed or pass a red light. Obey posted signs, traffic laws, temporary restrictions and directions from authorities. Adjust your speed for conditions. Set up, test and report only while safely parked; do not react abruptly to the siren or use a volume that masks traffic and emergency vehicles.

The app does not detect hardware or confirm whether a camera is operating. Cameras and limits may be missing, estimated, outdated or wrong. GPS, permissions, battery settings, iOS restrictions, calls and audio connections can delay or prevent alerts. Silence does not mean there is no camera or that your speed is lawful.
